title: First login

---

After the installation finishes, Notploy is running but has no administrator yet. The first visit creates one.

## Complete the initial setup

1. Open `http://<your-server-ip>:3000` in a browser.
2. On the setup page, create the **administrator account** — this becomes the owner of your organization.
3. Sign in with the credentials you just created.

**Expected result:** you land on the dashboard, signed in, with an empty organization ready for its first project.

<Callout type="warn" title="You cannot reach the setup page">
Confirm the server firewall allows port `3000` and that the Notploy containers are running.
See [Notploy instance troubleshooting](/docs/core/troubleshooting/instance).
</Callout>

## What you are now

The account you created is the organization **owner**: it can manage projects, resources and members. Additional members are invited later with roles that limit what they can do — see [Permissions](/docs/core/permissions).

## Secure the installation

Before you expose Notploy to the internet, do two things:

1. **Put the dashboard behind HTTPS.** Follow [Domain and TLS](/docs/core/domain-and-tls) to point a domain at the panel and enable a certificate.
2. **Then disable direct IP:port access** (optional but recommended). Once HTTPS for your panel domain is confirmed working, you can stop publishing port `3000` directly:

   ```bash
   docker service update --publish-rm "published=3000,target=3000,mode=host" notploy
   ```

<Callout type="warn">
Do **not** disable IP:port access before a working HTTPS domain is in place, or you will lock
yourself out of the dashboard. Verify first, remove access second.
</Callout>
